AI chip stock

Cumulative AI chips acquired since the start of 2022, in H100-equivalents, on a log scale. Estimates by Epoch AI, through its last complete quarter.

What "China" means here. It is Epoch's estimate of chips sold to companies based in China (Nvidia's H800, H20 and others, AMD's MI308X) plus Chinese-designed chips (Huawei Ascend, Cambricon). It does not mean government ownership. It excludes smuggled chips unless the smuggling estimate is switched on, and Epoch describes that estimate as speculative.

What the US line means. Epoch gives no US total. "Seven US companies" is the sum of the owners it names individually (Amazon, CoreWeave, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle and xAI), so it understates the US. "Everything outside China" adds Epoch's "Other" group, which mixes smaller US buyers with buyers in other countries. The lag is measured against the seven companies and is therefore a minimum.

Price against capability

Each point is one model configuration with a list price. The stepped lines are each country's price frontier: the cheapest way to get at least that score.

These are list prices. They show what a user pays per token, not what it costs to run the model. Some providers price below cost, and a model that uses more tokens per task costs more than its per-token price suggests.

    Methods and data

    How every number on this site is calculated, what counts, and what is still uncertain.

    One rule

    Take China's best result released by a date. Find the earliest US result, released by that date, that was at least as good. The lag is the time between that US result and the date. If no US result is as good, China leads, and the same rule runs in reverse to give a negative lag.

    • Capabilities: the result is a benchmark score, dated by the model's release. An area's lag is the median across its counted benchmarks.
    • Chip stock: the result is cumulative H100-equivalents at each quarter end, so the lag moves in three-month steps and is measured to Epoch's last complete quarter.
    • Best chip: the result is a chip's published 16-bit performance, dated by its release.
    • Training compute: the result is a model's estimated training compute, dated by its release.
    • Cost: the rule is not applied. Prices are known only for today, so the page compares them directly and records them daily to build a history.

    What counts

    • Counted benchmarks: at least 3 Chinese models released in the last 12 months and one in the last 6, and not saturated or closed.
    • Saturated: both countries' best within 5% of the ceiling, or on a hand-kept list of saturated benchmarks.
    • Low confidence: an area with fewer than 3 counted benchmarks, or whose benchmarks disagree on direction. Shown faded, never hidden, and left out of headlines.
    • Published estimates: shown beside an area with fewer than 2 counted benchmarks, labelled with publisher, date and what they measure.
    • Independent sources: results not reported by the model's own developer. The toggle on the capabilities tab switches to all sources.
    • Before 2025: shaded on every time chart, because few Chinese models, and almost no closed ones, were scored.

    Caveats specific to compute and cost

    • The China chip bucket is chips Epoch infers were sold to China-based companies, plus Chinese-designed chips. It is not government ownership and excludes smuggled chips unless that estimate is switched on.
    • The smuggling estimate is Epoch's own model output and Epoch calls it speculative. It starts in the first quarter of 2024.
    • No US total exists in the chip data. Seven named US companies stand in for the US, which understates it and makes the lag a minimum.
    • Chip performance is a specification, not a count of chips made. A chip that was announced but produced in small numbers counts the same as one produced in millions.
    • Training compute has disclosure bias: most recent US closed models have no estimate.
    • Prices are list prices, not costs, and the history starts on the day this view was added.
